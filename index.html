Selected Projects

Project 01 · Remote Sensing & Hydrological Monitoring

Climate-Driven Lake Dynamics Analysis using Remote Sensing and ERA5 Data

A geospatial research project investigating how major global lakes respond to drought periods. The project integrates satellite altimetry, ERA5 climate reanalysis data, DEM-based lake morphology and surface-water extent information to analyze long-term lake-level variability and hydrological change.

The work focuses on building a multi-source geospatial workflow for monitoring lake dynamics across different climatic regions. It combines spatial visualization, time-series analysis and hydrological interpretation to compare the drought responses of large inland water systems.

English Thesis Title:
Lakes’ Response within Drought Periods.

Study Area:
Lake Victoria, Lake Michigan, Lake Malawi and Lake Urmia across Africa, North America and Asia.

Data:
Satellite radar altimetry, HydroSat lake surface-water extent, ERA5 precipitation and evapotranspiration, DEM-based morphology data.

Methods:
ArcGIS, QGIS, remote sensing, geospatial visualization, hydrological monitoring, statistical time-series analysis.

Core Outputs:
Global lake maps, lake-level anomaly curves, volume-change time series and comparative drought-response visualizations.

Key Findings:
Lake-level anomalies and volume changes show region-specific responses to drought, influenced by basin-scale precipitation and evapotranspiration dynamics.

Project 02 · Climate Analytics & Time-Series Modeling

Hydrological Drought Analysis & Climate Time-Series Modeling

A climate analytics project focused on quantifying hydrological drought conditions through lake-level anomalies, precipitation–evapotranspiration dynamics and standardized drought indices. The project evaluates how climate signals are reflected in the physical response of large lake systems.

This part emphasizes statistical trend estimation, drought classification and multi-variable time-series interpretation. It links meteorological drought indicators with geodetic lake observations to assess whether drought intensity, lake-level decline and basin-scale climate behavior are consistent across regions.

Research Focus:
Hydrological drought detection, climate-response modeling and lake-level anomaly interpretation.

Study Area:
Selected lake basins, with emphasis on Lake Malawi, Lake Victoria and their surrounding hydrological systems.

Data:
ERA5 climate reanalysis, SPI, SPEI, PDSI drought indices, satellite-derived water levels and basin-scale climate variables.

Methods:
Python, weighted least squares adjustment, drought classification, statistical trend estimation and multi-variable time-series analysis.

Core Outputs:
Drought dashboards, P-ET interaction plots, lake-level anomaly analysis and drought-period trend tables.

Key Findings:
Extreme drought phases often correspond to significant water-level anomalies, but the strength of correlation differs between lakes and climatic regions.
